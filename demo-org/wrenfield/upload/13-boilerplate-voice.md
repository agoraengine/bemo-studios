# Communications guide: boilerplate and voice

*The Wrenfield Alliance. Maintained by Theo Marsh, Communications Manager. Updated June 2026.*

## Standard boilerplate

Use this paragraph verbatim in press releases, grant attachments, and event programs:

> The Wrenfield Alliance helps young people and their families in Kessler County stay fed, stay housed, and stay on track in school. Founded in 2011, the Alliance runs three programs, After the Bell, The Pantry Route, and Home Base, with a staff of nine and more than 60 volunteers. Learn more at wrenfieldalliance.org.

Short version (social bios, sponsor decks):

> Helping Kessler County kids and families stay fed, stay housed, and stay on track in school since 2011.

Tagline: **Neighbors, not caseloads.**

## Voice rules

1. **Plain words.** We serve families, we do not "engage stakeholders." Kids are kids, not "youth populations." If a sentence would sound strange said out loud at a Pantry Route stop, rewrite it.
2. **Numbers with their asterisks.** Every impact number travels with its honest context. The 87% housing outcome always notes it measures families who completed the program. The 93% attendance rate always appears next to the 84% district average, and we say it has plateaued.
3. **People before programs.** Lead with a person or a moment, then the program, then the number. Never open with an acronym.
4. **Families own their stories.** No family's name, image, or story is used without consent, and they preview anything that quotes them. When in doubt, anonymize or cut.
5. **No rescue language.** We do not save, transform, or lift anyone. Our neighbors do the work; we hold the door. Words we do not use: needy, at-risk (say "kids in the program" or name the actual risk), underprivileged, giving back.

## Channels

- **Newsletter:** monthly, 4,800 subscribers, written by Theo, one story + one number + one ask (alternating volunteer/donate).
- **Social:** photos from real program moments, posted with consent. No stock photography, ever.
- **Annual report:** published each April. The ED letter always includes at least one thing that did not work.

## Audiences, in priority order

1. Program families (they read the newsletter too; write for them first)
2. Monthly donors and volunteers
3. Funders and prospective funders
4. Local press and the broader community

# The Wrenfield Alliance: 2025 Annual Report

*The Wrenfield Alliance for Youth and Families, Inc. Fiscal year January 1 to December 31, 2025. Published April 2026.*

## A letter from Dana

Neighbors,

In 2025 the Alliance served 1,240 young people, delivered 96,400 meals across 14 Pantry Route stops, and walked 214 families through Home Base. Those are the numbers. Here is what they meant on a Tuesday in November: a van pulled into the First Baptist lot at 4:40, ten minutes early, and a fourth grader who had been at After the Bell an hour earlier helped her grandmother carry a box to the car. Three programs, one family, one afternoon. That is the whole idea of this organization.

The honest news belongs in this letter too. Our Prairie Light Foundation letter of intent for a youth mental health pilot was declined; we will sharpen it and reapply in 2027. And school attendance among our After the Bell regulars, 93% against a district average of 84%, held steady rather than improving. We are proud of the gap and impatient with the plateau.

We closed the year with revenue of $1.42 million against expenses of $1.36 million and 3.2 months of operating reserve. We are stable, careful, and small on purpose.

Thank you for showing up with us.

Dana Whitaker, Executive Director

## Program highlights

### After the Bell

Our after-school learning and mentoring program for grades 4 to 8 ran at Wrenfield Middle School, Kessler Elementary, and our own Fourth Street center. 410 students attended in 2025. Students who came 60 days or more attended school at a 93% rate; the district average is 84%. Fifteen volunteer mentors gave every Tuesday and Thursday. Under our strategic plan we are holding at three sites and investing in quality: this year that meant a shared homework protocol across sites and our first alumni survey, going to families of former participants now in high school.

### The Pantry Route

Two vans, 14 weekly stops, no waiting room. The Pantry Route delivered 96,400 meals in 2025 in partnership with the Kessler County Food Bank. Rev. James Whitfield's congregation at Wrenfield First Baptist hosted two of our busiest stops. Our 38 volunteer drivers logged the equivalent of two circuits of the equator; Bert Lindqvist kept both vans running the whole way. The number we watch most is on-time arrival: 96% of stops opened within ten minutes of schedule, because a reliable van is the difference between a service and a promise.

### Home Base

Home Base pairs emergency rent and utility assistance (average: $1,140 per family) with twelve months of case management. In 2025 we served 214 families, and 87% of families who completed the program were still stably housed a year later. The Kessler County family stability contract funded $95,000 of this work, and the county renewed the contract in May 2026 on the strength of these results.

## The Harvest Light Gala

Our October gala at the Wrenfield Armory drew 340 guests and netted $164,000, our best result yet. Honorary chair Eleanor Voss opened the evening; Tamika Bell and her 12-member committee ran it to the minute. The spring Route Run 5K added $26,000 for the Pantry Route.

## Looking ahead: 2026

Three commitments from our 2025 to 2027 strategic plan guide the year: deepen rather than widen our programs, diversify funding so no single source exceeds 15% of revenue, and build the operational backbone so what we know does not depend on who is in the room. We will report against all three next year, plainly, whatever the numbers say.

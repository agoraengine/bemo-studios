# Press release: Kessler County renews family stability contract with The Wrenfield Alliance

FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE
May 12, 2026
Contact: Theo Marsh, Communications Manager, theo@wrenfieldalliance.org

**WRENFIELD, Ohio.** Kessler County has renewed its family stability contract with The Wrenfield Alliance, extending $95,000 in funding for the Alliance's Home Base program, county officials announced Tuesday.

Home Base pairs one-time emergency rent or utility assistance with twelve months of case management. In 2025 the program served 214 families, and 87 percent of families who completed it were still stably housed a year after exiting.

"The check stops the eviction, but the year of follow-through is what changes the trajectory," said Marcus Hale, who has directed Home Base since its launch in 2021. "The county saw that in the numbers and backed it. That is what this renewal means."

The renewal cited the program's twelve-month housing outcomes. The Alliance has said it will seek to convert the annual contract into a multi-year agreement.

"Two hundred fourteen families is not an abstraction in a county this size," said Dana Whitaker, the Alliance's executive director. "Those are our neighbors, and most of them have a kid in one of our other programs. Stable housing is the floor under everything else we do."

Home Base is one of three programs run by The Wrenfield Alliance for Youth and Families, alongside the After the Bell after-school program and The Pantry Route mobile food pantry. In 2025 the Alliance served 1,240 young people across Kessler County.

**About The Wrenfield Alliance:** The Wrenfield Alliance helps young people and their families in Kessler County stay fed, stay housed, and stay on track in school. Founded in 2011, the Alliance runs three programs with a staff of nine and more than 60 volunteers. Learn more at wrenfieldalliance.org.

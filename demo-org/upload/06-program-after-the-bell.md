# Program profile: After the Bell

*The Wrenfield Alliance. Updated June 2026.*

## What it is

After the Bell is our after-school learning and mentoring program for students in grades 4 to 8. It runs Monday through Thursday, 3:30 to 5:30, at three sites: Wrenfield Middle School, Kessler Elementary, and the Alliance's Fourth Street center. Every afternoon follows the same arc: snack, homework block with the shared homework protocol, then clubs or mentoring.

## Why it exists

The program started in 2012 when Wrenfield Middle's then-principal Ruth Ellison (now our board secretary) told our founders that the students struggling most were not failing at 10 a.m., they were disappearing at 3 p.m. After the Bell fills the hours between the last bell and a parent's shift end, the window when homework does not get done and trouble does.

## The numbers (FY2025)

- 410 students enrolled across three sites
- 15 volunteer mentors, each matched with 2 to 3 students, every Tuesday and Thursday
- Students attending 60+ program days attended school at a 93% rate vs. an 84% district average
- Cost per student: roughly $1,050 per year

## Who runs it

Jaylen Cross has directed the program since 2016 and came up through it: he was a mentor first. Lena Vasquez coordinates the school sites and family communication. Ruth Ellison still interviews every prospective mentor before Sam Petrie onboards them.

## How it is funded

The state afterschool expansion grant ($180,000 per year, year 2 of 3) carries the sites. The balance comes from general operating funds, including Copperline Foundation support. The grant's annual report is due each January; Jaylen and Corinne Delgado co-write it.

## What we are working on

Per the strategic plan, After the Bell is capped at three sites through 2027. The current investments are quality, not growth: the shared homework protocol rolled out in 2025, the first alumni survey went to families of former participants now at Wrenfield High (board member Ana Barros is our bridge there), and attendance among regulars is the outcome indicator we report monthly. The number has plateaued at 93% for two years; closing the last gap is the program's open question, and we say so out loud.

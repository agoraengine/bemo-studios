# Program profile: The Pantry Route

*The Wrenfield Alliance. Updated June 2026.*

## What it is

The Pantry Route is a mobile food pantry: two vans, 14 weekly stops across Kessler County, no waiting room, no paperwork at the curb. Families take what they need. Stops are scheduled where people already are: church parking lots, the laundromat on Route 9, two school pickup lines, and the mobile home park on the county's east edge.

## Why it exists

We launched the Route in 2016 after realizing our neighbors were not skipping the county food pantry because they did not need food; they were skipping it because it was eight miles away, open 10 to 2 on weekdays, and required a form. We moved the food to the people and dropped the form.

## The numbers (FY2025)

- 96,400 meals distributed
- 14 weekly stops; 96% opened within ten minutes of schedule
- 38 volunteer drivers
- Sourcing partner: Kessler County Food Bank, which supplies the food at roughly $0.18 per meal to us

## Who runs it

Nora Steinfeld has managed the Route since 2017 and built the stop network stop by stop. Sam Petrie coordinates the 38 drivers. Board member Bert Lindqvist maintains both vans himself and keeps a spare-parts shelf at the Fourth Street center. Rev. James Whitfield hosts two of the busiest stops at Wrenfield First Baptist.

## How it is funded

The Route runs on the cheapest dollars in the organization: food bank partnership pricing, volunteer drivers, and donated vehicle maintenance. Cash costs (fuel, insurance, staff time, cold storage) come from general operating funds and the spring Route Run 5K, which netted $26,000 in 2025. Hartwell Regional Bank employees drove volunteer shifts all summer as part of the bank's sponsorship relationship.

## What we are working on

On-time arrival is the outcome indicator: a reliable van is the promise, and 96% is good but not our ceiling. The open decision for 2026 is whether to add a 15th stop near Kessler Elementary or add a second weekly visit to the two highest-demand stops. Demand data says deepen; geography says widen; the strategic plan says deepen wins ties.

## What this video is for

Two jobs, deliberately in this order. First, seed real work product into the Common Table workspace: a press release built in the app's templates and document editor, so that the workspace stops looking staged and every future capture (Home, workspace shots, this clip) shows an organization with actual work in flight. Second, capture that work being made as a sizzle clip: a LinkedIn viewer watches a real document take shape in the real product, in the org's own voice, and comes away wanting to see it on their own organization. The clip gets the meeting; it is never the meeting.

This is the first of a possible series of work-seeding passes (Becky, 2026-08-06: "build some work in the templates and document editor - start with a press release"). Each later seed gets its own scope note in this folder or its own production.

## Who watches it, and in what context

Muted autoplay in a LinkedIn feed, argument carried by on-screen text, captions on (series contract in `productions/linkedin-sizzle-series/roster.md`). A stranger who has never seen BeMo watches a press release get drafted and recognizes their own Tuesday.

## What it must say

1. **"Every piece of content reflects who you actually are, not who you were three drafts ago."** Amplify lead message, GA message map 3.2. The press release sounds like Common Table because the platform holds Common Table's voice and facts.
2. **Problem first: press release day starts with reassembling, not writing.** The numbers live in a spreadsheet, the program history in someone's head, the boilerplate in whichever doc used it last. Traces to "most work isn't creating, it's reconstructing" (messaging-assets-v1, supporting claim 1) and the ratified 15-second problem statement.
3. **The draft starts from what the organization already knows.** "BeMo is where your organization's knowledge lives" made visible on screen: collected facts, KB inputs, the org's own names and numbers arriving in the draft. The roof, shown not asserted.

One pillar per piece: this is a Stop Starting Over / voice-expression clip (communications-lead audience per the message map's pillar table). Colleague and Legacy stay out.

## What it must not say

- **No personalized wow** (Amendment 2). The clip shows the shape of the work on a fictional org. It never implies "this is your organization"; the wow happens live.
- **No AI-led or feature-led framing** (anti-patterns). The technology is never the subject; the document is. Never "AI writes your press release." BeMo is not a content generator: the value on screen is the context arriving, not the output volume.
- **No replacement claims.** The press release is Common Table's work, drafted with the platform. Never "no comms manager needed."
- **No false simplicity or time claims**: no "in minutes," no "just," no counting seconds. Individual time outcomes belong to cleared stories, not to this clip.
- **Common Table is fictional and must read as demo, not as proof.** Provenance treatment per the video brand rules; nothing on screen or in copy may imply Common Table is a customer. No real-customer resemblance (demo-org README no-blur rule).
- **Screen hygiene:** every take starts a fresh document (pre-wipe docs carry another org's facts); the KB table's Owner column shows the real account name and stays off screen; product em dashes in chat output are a known product finding, avoid lingering frames on them.
- No em dashes, no banned AI-tell vocabulary, anywhere in narration or on-screen text.

# Brief: Ask It Something It Can't Know

**Status:** DRAFT
**Owner:** Becky
**Target length:** Each answer post is a screen-capture clip of 30 seconds or less, or a still set. The series has no fixed length; the format is the deliverable.
**Opened:** 2026-08-12

## What this video is for

Get skeptical nonprofit LinkedIn to test BeMo's honesty in public. The audience poses questions in the comments, Becky asks BeMo against the Common Table demo organization, and the next post shows the answer with its sources, or shows BeMo saying it does not know. The job is participation: strangers experience the product's defining behavior (sourced answers, honest gaps) in their feed, before any call, and the comment thread does the distribution. This production also carries the companion gaps artifact: the shareable "here is what BeMo said was missing from this organization's knowledge base" list, paired with a LinkedIn poll.

## Where it serves

GA launch Wave 2, the owned wave, during the on-ramp weeks between site-live (August 13) and the GA declaration. Arc register: recognition and reframing (see it thinking, see it admit what it lacks). It operationalizes two ratified message-map items: the trust-posture behavior claim "BeMo tells you when it doesn't know" (`ga-message-map.md` 2.3, validated in beta) and the gap-honesty pair "The front page shows what's in play. The Knowledge Base tells you what's missing" (v1.4, live on the site). Grew out of the Week 29 "ask it something it can't know" carousel concept (`../bemo-os/docs/communications/narrative-arc/anchors/2026-w29-asset-plan.md`).

## Who watches it, and in what context

Nonprofit EDs and development directors in Becky's LinkedIn orbit, in feed, muted, mid-scroll. Two moments: the invitation post (they decide whether to play) and the answer post the next day (they see whether the product was straight with them). Participants are named and thanked in the answer post, which gives them a reason to reshare.

## What it must say

1. The Knowledge Base tells you what's missing. (Gap-honesty pair, ratified through use, message map v1.4.)
2. BeMo tells you when it doesn't know. (Trust posture 2.3, publishable behavior claim validated in beta.)
3. Answers come with sources you can check. (Sourced-answers product behavior; trace against the claim map at script time before any narration asserts it.)

## What it must not say

- No personalized wow (Amendment 2). Every question runs against Common Table, the standing fictional organization. Questions about a commenter's real organization get a warm redirect to a call, never an on-screen answer.
- Never frame the answer as authoritative advice (anti-patterns: AI output is input to judgment, not a verdict). The register is "here is what it said and where that came from," not "here is what you should do."
- No staged theater. Invited first-round questioners (advisors, beta users) are fine, but they are named as invited, never dressed up as strangers.
- No urgency, no GA references, no countdown.
- LinkedIn posting is manual, always. Nothing in this series is automated.

## Success looks like

- By the second round, questions arrive from people outside the beta circle without being invited.
- Commenters describe the behavior correctly in their own words ("it tells you when it doesn't know").
- At least one answer post outperforms that week's scheduled reel in reach or comments.

# The Question Posters: a BeMo brand awareness campaign

**v8 · August 16, 2026** · DRAFT for Becky's ratification
**Owner:** Becky · **Built in:** productions/question-posters/

## The campaign idea in one line

Every week, BeMo asks nonprofits one question they cannot help answering, in a frame they learn to recognize before they read a word.

## What actually makes it stronger

Becky asked whether the lever is design, on-screen copy, or distribution. All three have jobs, and they are not equal:

1. **Distribution is the multiplier.** A perfect poster seen once builds nothing. The same frame at the same weekly moment, for twelve straight weeks, is what builds the memory. At BeMo's current following, the company page alone cannot carry that; Becky's and Lee's personal networks are the reach engine. The campaign is designed around that reality.
2. **Design is the recognition asset.** The fixed system (navy ground, bold white wordmark, green accent words, one gag, silence) is what a stranger recognizes on the third exposure. It never varies. Agencies call these distinctive brand assets; the discipline is that they are not creative choices per post, they are the uniform.
3. **On-screen copy is the message.** The question does the work of being remembered. It must be a pain the viewer recognizes as theirs in under a second, named in their words, never the category's.

Rank order: distribution consistency, then design consistency, then each week's question. The most common failure is inverting this: agonizing over copy while posting erratically from one low-follower page.

## The format, locked

Per poster: 6 to 8 seconds, silent, looping, 16:9 primary plus the 1080x1080 feed square (the square posts to LinkedIn; the 16:9 goes to the website and email). One held composition: the question in giant white type with one or two green words, exactly one word acting out its meaning, the bold wordmark with pulsing arcs, "Where missions gain momentum." and the bemointel.ai pill beneath. Built on the cut 5 rig; a new poster is roughly an hour of work.

"Nothing falls on the floor. And nothing walks out the door." stays in rotation as the statement poster, the campaign's exclamation point rather than its weekly voice.

## The sound grammar (ratified by Becky, Aug 15)

**The sound is the action, or there is no sound.** No music: a bed on a short loop reads as an ad sting. Real samples only (licensed from the HeyGen catalog on Becky's plan). On Nothing Falls, the slip builds the expectation of a crash and the crash never comes, because the word is fall and nothing falls; then the soft catch, the real door shutting (just the shut, no air, per Becky's QA), and the chime. Typing under the retype, ticks under the counter, the door track on the door poster. The near-silent gags (a fade, a blink, a strike) carry only **the chime on the arc reveal, which every poster shares as the campaign's one sonic signature.** The muted feed never hears any of it; the sound-on minority gets the same joke the animation tells.

## Yes, every poster posts with copy

The Apple mechanic is a pair: the post copy is the tease, the video is the reveal. A poster posted bare is half an asset. Copy rules: one or two sentences, problem-first, no product name in the hook, no GA countdown, no banned words, no em dashes. The link is bemointel.ai.

## Who posts what (the three-voice pattern)

LinkedIn is never automated; every post is placed by a person, per standing practice.

- **The BeMo company page is the home of record.** Every poster posts there first, Friday morning. The page becomes the campaign's archive: scroll it and you see the uniform.
- **Becky reposts the same day, early afternoon, with one personal line above it.** Not the company copy; her voice, org-first framing per the Aug 10 ruling (the video is BeMo's, the framing is hers). Example for poster 1: "I have heard some version of this from every ED I know." Becky's network is the campaign's largest audience; this step is not optional if the campaign is to work.
- **Lee alternates weeks** with the same pattern in his voice, so the two founder feeds are not identical mirrors. His weeks off, he reacts and comments instead.
- **Jon uses posters one-to-one**: a poster is a better conversation opener in a DM than a deck. No public posting role.

Nobody posts identical copy. Three identical posts in one day reads as astroturf to both people and the feed.

## Calendar: Friday, by the swap Becky reviewed Aug 15

The live weekly calendar (Becky, Aug 15): Sunday Field Notes video, Monday Field Notes anchor, Tuesday product reel, Wednesday Build Notes and carousel, Thursday feature reel, Friday quote card or still, Saturday open. Becky proposed Saturday for the posters; the agreed adjustment is **the Friday swap: question poster Friday, quote card or still Saturday.**

Why the swap: the poster is the stranger-stopping asset, and its whole mechanism is reach, so it belongs on a weekday; Friday has full weekday traffic with the week's lightest brand competition. The founder repost (the reach engine) needs a workday feed. And the quote card survives Saturday best of anything on the calendar: warm proof reads well in the Saturday morning catch-up scroll and does not depend on algorithmic push. The week then closes its arguing on Friday and rests on proof.

Load note, named honestly: seven posts a week is a real calendar. The poster is the cheapest asset on it (about an hour each with the rig). If the week ever needs to shed weight, the campaign drops to biweekly rather than moving days; consistency of the slot matters more than which slot.

## Measurement (honest for our size)

Brand awareness at this scale is measured in proxies, gathered manually:

- Company page: follower growth and profile visits, weekly, hand-logged.
- Per poster: impressions, 3-second views, completion if LinkedIn surfaces it, and saves/reposts (the recognition signals that matter more than likes).
- The real test: cold-call and demo-call anecdotes. When a prospect says "I keep seeing your blue posts," the campaign is working. Jon logs these.
- Review at six posters: keep, adjust cadence, or stop. Wear-out is real; the uniform stays, questions rotate, and the moment engagement decays two weeks running, the cadence slows rather than the design changing.

## What this campaign never does

No product shots, no feature names, no proof or customer names, no GA countdowns, no urgency, no answering its own question. The poster asks; the site answers. A poster that starts explaining is a feature reel wearing the wrong uniform.

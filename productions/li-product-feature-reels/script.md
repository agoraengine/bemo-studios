### The ordering ruling: name the product before the demo, not after

**Becky, August 21:** it does not make sense to a first-time viewer, and it has to be
clearer about what Compass is.

She is right, and the fault is structural rather than a matter of wording. The slate's
grammar puts the identity card near the end, which works for a viewer who already knows
BeMo. Read cold, this reel ran sixteen seconds of an unexplained chat interface before
the word Compass ever appeared, **and the 15-second cut never said it at all.** A
stranger scrolling the feed saw a pain line, two screenshots of text, and a logo, and
was never told what the product was, what it was called, or what category it is in.

Two changes, and they apply to both cuts:

1. **The identity card moves to second position**, straight after the problem, so the
   viewer knows what they are looking at before they look at it.
2. **It carries a plain description instead of the category line.** "One of four apps
   that share the same memory" is the ratified category line and it stays, at the end of
   the parent, but it tells a newcomer nothing about what Compass does. The card now
   reads **"Compass. A thinking partner for strategy that remembers what your
   organization already decided, and why."** That is the live site's own sentence from
   the Compass page ("a thinking partner for strategy that preserves the reasoning
   behind every decision"), which is the point: a viewer who watches this and then lands
   on bemointel.ai should hear the same thing twice.

The four-dimensions caption also goes plain in these cuts. "Four lenses you do not have
four people for" is the better line and it stays on the record, but it describes an
absence rather than what the viewer is watching happen. The v3 cuts run **"It answers
with your numbers, your history, your board."**, which tracks the site's "BeMo answers
with your objective, your numbers, your history" and describes what is on screen.

# The Amplify template slate

**Status:** DRAFT, August 14, 2026. Six reels sharing the Amplify reel's grammar, so the slate reads as a series.
**Chosen from** the template shelf as observed August 14. The full shelf runs past the fold, so this is the top six by expected use rather than a complete ranking.

## How they were chosen

Two filters, applied in order. **Frequency:** what a small nonprofit actually opens most, which is dominated by donor communications rather than the showpiece documents. **Different behaviour:** six near-identical letter flows would be six near-identical reels, so each one has to demonstrate something the others do not.

| # | Template | Why it is in the six | What it demonstrates that the others do not |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Donor Thank You Letter** | The highest-frequency writing job in the sector. Every gift needs one, they are time-sensitive, and they are the first thing to slip when a week gets away from someone | Volume and speed. The job that is never behind by choice |
| 2 | **Annual Report** | The biggest annual lift, and the one place we already have a customer's own account of the before and after | Scale: the multi-day document that becomes an afternoon |
| 3 | **Impact Story** | What every funder asks for and what most organizations find hardest to write about themselves | Pulling program outcomes out of the knowledge base rather than out of memory |
| 4 | **Press Release** | Occasional, but high-stakes and highly visible, and we already hold footage of this exact flow | Boilerplate and quotes assembled from the record, correct without being looked up |
| 5 | **Fact Sheet** | The document that gets reused constantly: board packets, funder meetings, new staff | The knowledge base made into a page. The closest thing to the roof claim in one screen |
| 6 | **Elevator Pitch** | The fastest possible win, and the one a skeptic will try first because it costs them nothing | Voice consistency: the same organization sounding like itself in thirty seconds |

Deliberately not in the six: Action Alert and Crowdfunding Campaign Page (advocacy and campaign-specific, narrower than the ICP), Donor Recognition List and Donor Update Letter (real but close cousins of 1, and the slate does not need three donor letters), Blog Post (frequent, but it is the least differentiated thing an AI writing tool can show, and showing it invites exactly the "content generator" comparison the anti-patterns forbid).

## The shared grammar

Every reel in the slate runs the same shape, which is what makes it a series:

| 3.5 to 9.5 | Beat one, captioned. The product doing the thing nobody expects it to do |

| 14.5 | End of the fifteen-second core |
| 14.5 to 22.5 | Cleared proof if we have it for that surface, otherwise a third product beat |
| 22.5 to 26 | The Amplify identity card, icon and green |
| 26 to 30 | The standing close |

Captions and music, no voiceover. Corner wordmark throughout. ~~Music bed A.~~ **Beds updated (Becky, 2026-08-16, from the August 15 audition): the 30-second parents ride bed P2 (confident) and the 15-second cores ride bed P1 (saas demo); bed A is superseded for this slate. Same ruling: the 15-second core is the LinkedIn feed asset and the 30s parent posts to YouTube.** Remixed by `capture/mix-beds.mjs` from the kept pictures.

**The push-in rule (Becky, August 15, 2026):** app footage never sits small in a full-page frame. Every product beat carries a slow zoom into the document editor so the work being done fills the screen, rendered supersampled so the type stays crisp. The same rule applies to any reel where the meaningful screen area is smaller than the frame.

**The sound rules for voiced cuts (ratified through the August 15 fixes; the mixer of record is `capture/mix-vo2.mjs` and it enforces most of these):**

1. **Voice first.** The voice is the master track. The bed ducks about 7dB under every spoken word by sidechain (fast attack, ~400ms release) and breathes back between lines. Never the reverse, and never a shared level.
2. **No dynamic loudness normalization anywhere in the chain.** It pumps the whole mix down when the music enters and swallows the voice. Static measured gains per line (target -17 LUFS each; renders arrive 8 to 12dB quiet, so expect real lift), a limiter, then one static trim to the -16 LUFS house target.
3. **Speech is never trimmed.** Not tails, not decays. If a line needs room, the picture gives it room: the close holds longer, or the line gets rewritten shorter and regenerated.
4. **Placements come from measured read lengths, never estimates.** The mixer aborts on any overlap between lines or any line running past the cut. An aborted mix is a script or placement problem, not a filter problem.
5. **Beds run at native length only.** Never loop a track with a composed outro; it dies mid-cut and slams back in. Stretch up to about 3 percent is fine; beyond that, source a bed at the right length.
6. **Quotes are read in silence.** Narration may introduce them ("One user put it better than we could") but never speaks another person's first-person words, which would misattribute them to the narrator's voice.
7. **QA is a waveform, looked at.** Before anything reaches review: draw the full waveform and check every line lands as a distinct cluster with air around it, and measure speech windows at least 10dB over music-only passages. Numbers alone missed four audible faults in one day; the picture caught them all.

**Captions sit at the top of the frame** (Becky, August 15): player controls cover the bottom.

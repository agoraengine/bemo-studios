## What this video is for

Open a door. A nonprofit ED or development director who has never heard of BeMo watches 60 seconds and comes away wanting a conversation. It is the asset Jon sends before a meeting, the asset Bill can forward into his network, and the piece that debuts publicly in the owned wave.

It is not a demo. Its entire job is to make the live session worth booking.

## Where it serves

- **Wave 0** of [`ga-launch/00-overview.md`](../../../bemo-os/docs/initiatives/ga-launch/00-overview.md), which lists "sizzle reel script and shot list" as Becky plus Lee, not started. Wave 0's exit criteria is that a stranger handed the press kit, the sizzle reel, and the website describes BeMo correctly.
- **Wave 2**, where it debuts publicly as a post rather than an ad.
- Requested by both Jon and Bill, which is what prompted Amendment 2.

## Who watches it, and in what context

Two audiences, one video.

1. **Cold, muted, mid-scroll on LinkedIn.** Captions carry it. The first three seconds decide everything. This is the harder case and the video is built for it.
2. **Warm, sent by Jon or an affiliate ahead of a call.** They already have a reason to watch. This audience is forgiving, so designing for the cold case costs nothing here.

The ICP is small-to-mid nonprofits. The viewer is competent, overloaded, and has been sold to by software companies before.

## What it must say

Three points, from [`messaging-assets-v1.md`](../../../bemo-os/docs/internal/initiatives/messaging-assets-v1.md).

1. **The work keeps happening, but it does not build.** Processes live in people's heads. Grant narratives get rebuilt every cycle. When someone leaves, the organization starts over. This is a structural problem, not a people problem.
2. **BeMo is where the organization's knowledge lives.** Strategy, funding, communications, and learning on one foundation that remembers.
3. **It is not stateless.** Unlike a general AI tool, session 500 knows your organization. That is the difference the viewer needs to feel, not be told.

Institutional knowledge leads. Coordination language stays in the structural layer and never surfaces as the headline, per the July 29 messaging decisions.

## What it must not say

- **No attempt at the personalized wow.** Amendment 2 is explicit: this video shows the shape of the product and the problem it solves. The wow is the prospect's own organization on screen and that only exists live. Sizzle opens the door, the live session walks through it.
- **No "coordination collapse."** Internal diagnostic language. Becky flagged it on July 28 specifically because it was reading as external copy.
- **No feature tour.** Four products in 60 seconds is a website page, not a video.
- **No roadmap shown as shipped.** Allocate, agents, and anything behind the two-surfaces build stay out unless they are live at capture.
- **No named customer** unless cleared below.
- **No AI-company visual language.** No neural networks, no glowing orbs, no abstract data particles.

## What changes, and why

Six deltas between the July 30 draft and this brief. Ordered by how much work each one is.

**1. The Meg quote stays, and gets attributed.** The draft's quote section opens on "BeMo is the best colleague," unattributed. It keeps its place and gains her name. The per-use gate applies to the export, not the script: see The Meg gate above.

**2. Every quote gets a name.** The draft attributes everything to "From beta users" and carries a footer reading "approvals pending." Both go. Jen Allen, MaryEllen O'Donohue, and Meg Poe appear by name, role, and organization.

This is now a change the website needs too, not just the video. The site currently uses role and organization without names ("Executive Director, Autoimmune Encephalitis Alliance"). With the attribution decision made, that is a divergence the site should close. **Raise with Lee:** it is a copy change in `bemo-website`, not something Studios does. Logged in `findings.md`.

**3. The close is a beta close, not a GA close.** "In beta now" ships in a video whose job is the GA launch. The CTA needs to become the free tier for the Wave 2 cut and a conversation for the version Jon sends.

**4. It is not a video file.** The artifact is HTML with CSS animation. Jon cannot send it, it cannot post to LinkedIn, and it cannot be emailed. It has to be rendered to mp4.

**This is the useful discovery:** the artifact is itself capturable. Playwright can load it at 2560x1440, let the animation play, and record the whole thing. That is one capture run against a page that already exists, with no dependency on the two-surfaces build. The rendering problem is solved and the blocker is smaller than it looked.

**5. It misses the production standards.** 1280x720 against a 1920x1080 delivery spec, no burned-in captions, no vertical cut, no `.srt`. Captions matter most: the primary audience is muted and mid-scroll.

**6. The simulated-product problem.** The draft's footer says "product scenes are simulated (Common Table Food Pantry is fictional)." A fictional org is fine and correct, since a real org's data on screen is a permission problem. But hard rule 4 says no mockups presented as product, and a footer on the artifact page does not travel with an mp4.

Three ways out, in order of preference:

1. **Capture the real product** with a seeded demo org named Common Table. Best answer, gated on the two-surfaces build.
2. **Capture the real website**, which is live and capturable today, for any scene that can be carried by site UI rather than app UI.
3. **Keep the simulation and disclose in-frame**, a persistent corner label reading "Product simulation." Honest, and it costs a little polish. Acceptable for an interim cut; not acceptable for the version that runs in Wave 2.

**Open for Lee and Becky:** which of the three, and whether an interim cut under option 3 goes out to Jon now while we wait for real footage.
